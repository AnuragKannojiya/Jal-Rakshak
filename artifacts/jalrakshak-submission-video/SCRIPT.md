# JalRakshak — Competition Submission Film

## Director's treatment

- **Promise:** Make local water concerns visible, actionable, and trackable through one civic feedback loop.
- **Identity:** JalRakshak's midnight navy and clear-water cyan, with warm white for legible civic information. Sora display typography paired with Inter body copy.
- **Hero:** A water signal changing form: ripple → citizen report → area map → accountable response.
- **Motion:** Waterline match cuts, map-node tracing, and crisp UI assembly. Use one deliberate hold for comprehension, then a more decisive end-card reveal.
- **Arc:** An urgent but measured opening becomes an understandable service workflow, then resolves into an implementation plan and evaluation framework.
- **Payoff:** The closing changes from interface detail to a bright, open water field with the platform name and SDG 6 alignment.
- **Format and runtime:** 16:9 landscape, 50 seconds total. No voiceover; all essential information is on screen. One instrumental bed is planned; musical accent alignment remains unverified until the track exists.

## Complete shot-by-shot script

### Shot 01 / 00.00–09.00s / 9.00s — “Make the signal visible”

**Purpose:** Establish the practical problem without implying that any specific community's water is unsafe: local water concerns can be difficult to see, report, and follow through.

**Composition and artistic idea:** Start with a close, dark navy field. A thin cyan waterline enters from the upper right; its concentric rings resolve into a simplified neighborhood grid. The grid is the subject, not a background decoration. Keep the main type in the left safe zone and the expanding ripple on the right, then let the ripple overtake the frame. No stock-looking distressed-water imagery.

**Exact on-screen copy and holds:**
- 00.20–02.30s: “WATER ISSUES ARE LOCAL.”
- 02.45–04.70s: “THE SIGNAL SHOULDN’T BE.”
- 05.05–08.35s: “Make every concern visible.”
- 06.00–08.35s, small label: “JALRAKSHAK · CIVIC WATER QUALITY”

**Timed choreography:**
- 00.00–00.20s: Begin on a visible cyan point and partial ripple; no blank incoming frame.
- 00.20–00.65s: First ripple snaps outward. Headline line one punches in with a short, stiff scale settle.
- 00.65–01.10s: Three small location nodes appear at staggered 0.45-second intervals.
- 01.10–02.30s: Neighborhood grid draws outward from the water point; first line holds.
- 02.45–03.00s: “THE SIGNAL” reveals through a horizontal mask; “SHOULDN’T BE.” follows 0.25 seconds later.
- 03.00–04.70s: The grid nodes pulse in a path from the right edge toward the center; copy remains readable.
- 04.90–05.05s: Hard brightness lift on the center node.
- 05.05–05.55s: The final line resolves in the left third as the center node becomes a map pin.
- 05.55–07.75s: Camera pushes into the pin; its cyan ring ripples across the grid while the small platform label settles.
- 07.75–09.00s: The pin's center opens into a rounded rectangular aperture. The outgoing route line is already visible and continues into the report interface.

**Transition contract:** The central map pin becomes the location marker on the next shot's citizen report. The line crossing its lower edge continues as the next composition's map route.

**Assets and technique:** Original vector grid, ripple rings, and pin built as SVG/CSS geometry; Sora and Inter loaded from the existing font provider. No external footage or real-world incident imagery.

**Sound intent:** Instrumental opens with a restrained pulse and a single soft tonal lift around 05s. No voiceover or SFX. Accent placement is planned, not verified.

## Pre-code review

- **Distinct shots:** Macro radial field, portrait report UI, wide route map, warm horizontal rollout, and bright centered close differ in scale, palette, composition, and dominant motion.
- **Substantial motion:** Each shot has more than one timed event after its opening reveal; the final 15–20% includes an intentional handoff or loop move.
- **Visual ideas:** Ripple becomes neighborhood grid; map pin opens into a report; route demonstrates the response loop; a route node becomes a rollout plan; measurement ring resolves into the brand close.
- **Readability:** Headlines, interface labels, and supporting statements have separate zones. Long copy is confined to the roadmap and measurement shots and receives a stable read interval.
- **Progression:** Problem → report → response → implementation → evaluation. No unverified outcome statistic is used.
- **Feasibility:** All imagery is vector/interface geometry with CSS and SVG motion; no WebGL, complex external footage, or generated text in images is required.
